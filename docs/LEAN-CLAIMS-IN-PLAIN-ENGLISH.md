# What the Lean claims, in plain English

Generated overview of 187 headline Lean claims (the deployment verifier stack plus the headline theorems of scenarios SC-01 to SC-28). Each row is the first sentence of a full block
(the statement, its hypotheses, what it does **not** say, its kind, and whether its hypotheses are shown satisfiable) in `docs/lean-english/part-g0.md` .. `part-g7.md`.
The blocks were written by Sonnet from the Lean source (not from the scenario descriptions) and are **not yet verified one-to-one against the Lean**; that check is the next step
(`docs/lean-english/ONE-TO-ONE.md`). Treat every sentence as a claim to be checked, not as a result.

**One-to-one freshness as of draft PR #56:** G0 had 26 historical gate-checked comparisons, but the exercise was not strictly blind; revised G0 blocks (including `runtime_trace` and the earlier `Accepted` correction) need a new blind evaluation. **G1–G7 (161 claims) have never been checked one-to-one.** This is model-written, model-reviewed work; no independent human review has occurred.

**How to read the "Kind" column.** *general theorem*: a statement quantified over arbitrary instances. *finite computation*: checked by `decide` on one concrete case. *witness / non-vacuity example*: shows hypotheses can hold together. *negative control*: shows a weakened model fails. *refinement/bridge*: links a concrete machine to the abstract model. *definitional/trivial*: a projection or a restatement of a hypothesis.

**What a Lean theorem can and cannot give you.** Every claim is about a *model* (a list of operations and a state machine, or a finite graph). The runtime link (the real code does what the model does), extraction faithfulness (the model lists every real authority edge), and the trusted base (kernel, Docker, SQLite, Python, the hash function) are premises, never conclusions of these theorems.

## Claims the translators flagged as most likely to be overstated

These are the translators' own judgement, to be confirmed or refuted in the verification step.

1. **`Deployment.deployment_broker_safe` / `broker_contract`.** The harm is "reviewed, exactly approved, once-consumed release". The conclusion only says each published nonce has an approval marked *used* (plus no duplicates, count within the cap). It does not say who signed it, that the digest matched, or that a review happened; those are guards of the model's `release` step, not facts proved about reachable states. `Safe` is only counting and uniqueness. (A reachable-state provenance invariant was drafted but not built; see the verification task.)
2. **`Deployment.exclusive_sink`, `runtime_exclusive`, `accepted_no_avoided_path`.** Hold only over the edges the IR lists; edges into the broker or receiver are cut by definition; `exclusive_sink` covers only edges flagged `authority`.
3. **`TranscriptReplay.runtime_trace`, `final_safe`.** "Runtime" is a hand-mapped, trusted log shown to be a valid model chain; a truncated log also passes (`omitted_tail_is_accepted`).
4. **SC-26 `sc26_once`, `halt_freezes`.** Only each request id is reserved once (approvals are not consumed; the same payload under a new id can be paid again); in-flight messages can still land after HALT.
5. **SC-25 `sc25_safe`.** A later FAIL on the same digest does not block execution; role lists are unconstrained.
6. **SC-06 `sc06_safe`.** "Confirmed by a user" only means a confirm operation carrying a user id; typed fields are unbounded numbers, so an instruction can still ride in one.
7. **SC-07 `sc07_mixed`, `concrete_safe`.** Bounds each window, not the run total; the bound is on "never caught and capped total ≥ S", not "S bits before the first catch".
8. **SC-15 `sc15_safe`, SC-16 `sc16_safe`, SC-17 `sc17_safe`.** Hash match is not content identity without injectivity; "live equals desired" holds by construction; the diff in the operation is treated as trusted.
9. **SC-18 `concrete_safe`, SC-19 `sc19_safe`, SC-20 `sc20_safe`.** The conclusion drops part of what the guard enforces (promoter ≠ writer; allowlist); "verified snapshot" is a flag, not a restore test.
10. **SC-21 `sc21_safe`, `sc21_undetected_prob`; SC-24 `collusion_uniform`.** Escape probabilities, not detection guarantees; the bound is one-shot with a 50/50 prior and is vacuous at TV=1.
11. **SC-23 `sc23_safe`, isolation theorems.** "Untrusted" is the model's own provenance bit; a confirmation can back the same action repeatedly.
12. **SC-27 `sc27_safe`, `sc27_ext_safe`.** Truncation back to an earlier anchor is accepted; entries fabricated before the next anchoring get anchored.
13. **SC-28 `sc28_safe`, `exclusive_placement_safe`.** The meter charging at least real consumption is built into the model; escape from a cgroup is not expressible.
14. **SC-08..SC-14 (`exclusive_dispatcher`, `anti_rollback`, SC-10/11/12 policy theorems).** Several are true by construction of the model (the application is defined as everything except dispatch; no mounts or filesystem in the SC-11 model; unregistered creation is a no-op when `registryOnly` is on).
15. **`*_safe_authenticated` family.** The adversary is not constrained to issue only from the stated set; "not in U" follows from the hypothesis on U, so authentication adds little beyond the unauthenticated theorem on a filtered trace.

## DEPLOY: Deployment verifier: accepted configuration + broker protocol model + transcript replay

**Harm.** An agent causes a file to appear in the protected sink that is not attributable to a reviewed, exactly approved, once-consumed release within a shared budget, or an agent/deputy has an authority path to the sink or database that avoids the broker/receiver.

**In short.** There are three separable layers. (1) If a hand-built finite graph (`IR`) passes the `Accepted` checks, then no chain of edges in that graph lets an agent node reach the sink or database without passing through the broker or receiver. The sink and database authority edges all originate at the receiver or broker, and no agent shares the receiver's UID. (2) Two abstract protocol models, an SC26 "gate and bank" model and the `TrustedBroker` SQLite-transaction model, satisfy budget, key-uniqueness and one-use invariants over every finite sequence of model steps. (3) A single recorded 10-step transcript is replayed as a chain of model steps, and one of its replayed denials is shown to be a genuine model denial. The theorems that join these layers to the real sink (`deployment_safe`, `deployment_broker_safe`) take the graph being complete (`ExtractionFaithful`) and the real sink publications matching the model (`ProtocolRefinement` / `BrokerProtocolRefinement`) as hypotheses. The "published sink nonces are a sublist of consumed nonces" and "caller uid is an agent node's uid" facts are inputs, not outputs. The conclusions say a used approval exists for each published nonce. They do not say who signed it, that it matched the file's digest, or that the artifact was reviewed. Those facts are in the broker model's release guard but are not surfaced in the final theorem statements. World assumptions from the scenario, all `NOT_PROVED`: **faithful_extraction** (the IR contains every authority edge the real deployment has); **protocol_refinement** (the Python broker and receiver implement the Lean transition model, and sink files correspond to published nonces); **tcb** (kernel, Docker daemon reports, SQLite, Python, scanner and serializer are trusted). A separate, newly integrated model theorem (`Deployment.Provenance.deployment_broker_provenance`) establishes that each published nonce's final stored used approval has an approver-role signer, an agent-role owner, a staged digest, some current reviewer-role entry for that digest and an owner-matching lease, **assuming `BrokerProtocolRefinement`**. It does **not** establish immutable per-release reviewer identity, signature verification, digest/destination matching of published file bytes, or faithfulness of the running Python/SQLite implementation. This supplementary theorem is not one of the original 187 translated headlines; its English has not had a one-to-one check.

## SC-01: Lifetime-bounded exact secret recovery

**Harm.** A machine receiver recovers a uniform 32-bit secret with probability above 2^-16.

**In short.** The three theorems give a counting bound: if the receiver's entire observation over a lineage's lifetime takes at most 9^5 = 59049 values (five episodes, nine possible padded views each), then no sender/receiver pair with a shared seed recovers a uniform 32-bit secret with probability above 59049/2^32 (about 2^-16 after rounding up); `sc01_case` additionally bundles a side-observation version of the bound, a ledger lemma, and some usefulness arithmetic. Everything that connects this counting to a real VM is an assumption: that the receiver sees nothing except the five padded views (no timing or other side channel, `complete_receiver_observations`, NOT_PROVED), that any side channel has a domination certificate of total mass at most 65536/59049 (`side_kernel`), that the durable ledger really behaves like the Lean ledger (`ledger_correspondence`), that lifetimes are independent (`independent_lifetimes`) and that the honest task meets its floor on the VM host (`vm_usefulness`). Note that several assumptions the scenario lists as THEOREM_VERIFIED are, in Lean, only conjuncts of `sc01_case` about the Lean model (or about numbers), not statements about the deployed system.

| Lean claim | In plain English (first sentence) | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| `SafetyCaseSC01.sc01_case` | A conjunction of five separate facts about the SC-01 repair design. | general theorem |
| `GatewayCore.core_bound` | Model the gateway as a state machine over raw events (send a raw payload on a channel or on an unknown channel, any other side action, close). | general theorem |
| `Covert.covert_bound_lifetime` | Suppose the receiver's entire observation over the secret's lifetime is a tuple of E episode views, where episode e's view takes values in a finite set `V e`. | general theorem |

## SC-02: Sender communicates across multiple episodes to a persistent receiver

**Harm.** A persistent receiver recovers a k-bit secret across E episodes; the claim is that success probability is at most (product of per-episode certificate masses L_e) / 2^k.

**In short.** `sc02_accumulated` multiplies per-round (here "episode") certificate masses and divides by the secret space; `rekey_epoch` shows that if rounds outside an epoch are dominated by a certificate of mass at most 1, only the in-epoch masses count. Both are conditional on the receiver's per-round view being dominated by a certificate of the stated mass; Lean does not derive that domination from anything, so "measure or enforce L_e" (`episode_certificates`, NOT_PROVED) is the entire connection to a real system. The independence premise (`rekey_independence`, NOT_PROVED) appears in Lean only as the hypothesis `hout` (outside-epoch mass at most 1), which is a condition on the certificate, not a proof that no persistent state carries the secret. The proven bound is on exact recovery by the decoder, which is the "adversary's success" rather than any broader notion of "communication".

|---|---|---|
| `ChannelInstances.sc02_accumulated` | Consider E rounds. | general theorem |
| `ChannelInstances.rekey_epoch` | Same setting as above, but with a total of n rounds, and a window of E rounds starting at round a (requiring a + E at most n). | general theorem |

## SC-03: Pinned immediate-peer egress confinement

**Harm.** An untrusted sandbox process initiates an unauthorised immediate outbound peer connection.

**In short.** The Lean model is a small state machine: a sandbox can only cause outbound socket attempts via `directAttempt`, and the model's `step` makes such an attempt have any effect only when the launched topology is not "safe" (all five boolean flags true); every attempt that does occur through a request goes to a peer found in the pinned policy by `lookup`. Both theorems say the invariant "if active the topology is safe, and every recorded attempt came from the gateway to a pinned peer" is preserved by any action sequence. This is close to true by construction: the model's guards are exactly what make the invariant inductive, so the real content is in whether the real Linux setup corresponds to the flags (the topology booleans, policy integrity, exclusive mediation, attribution), which Lean does not touch (`policy_integrity` and `general_attribution` NOT_PROVED; `usefulness_population` NOT_PROVED).

|---|---|---|
| `EgressGate.step_safe` | For any fixed policy `p` (a list of rules, each pairing an authority string with a pinned peer address/port/protocol), any state `s`, and any single action `a`: if the state satisfies the invariant, so does the state after the action. | general theorem |
| `EgressGate.trace_safe` | For any policy, any state satisfying the invariant, and any finite list of actions, running the actions in order from that state ends in a state that also satisfies the invariant. | general theorem |

## SC-04: Agent leaks via logs, telemetry, diagnostic output, error strings or process timing

**Harm.** A receiver of logs/telemetry recovers a secret; the claim is probability at most K^m * Q^(f m) / |M| when the receiver sees only template ids and quantised fields.

**In short.** Both theorems are counting bounds: if what reaches the receiver in each of m rounds is one element of a fixed finite alphabet (K classes, or K templates with f fields at Q levels), then recovery of a uniform secret is at most (alphabet size)^m / |M|, even with hidden sender state and adaptive sender behaviour. The theorems have no content about whether a real logging pipeline outputs only such elements: that every receiver-visible diagnostic passes the trusted canonicaliser, and that timing and ordering are inside the view, are exactly the NOT_PROVED assumptions `canonicaliser_complete` and `timing_in_view`. The model also fixes the number of rounds m.

| `ChannelInstances.canonical_seq` | If each of m items reaches the monitor only as one of `Kc` canonical classes, then any adaptive generator (with hidden state and a shared seed) and any decoder recover a uniform message with probability at most Kc^m / /M/. | general theorem |

## SC-05: Agent coordinates a malicious action using a one-bit signal

**Harm.** Agents coordinate a prohibited action through a low-bandwidth (one-bit) signal.

**In short.** The three theorems establish a negative result, an equality, and a gate property: a cap on output size does not stop one-bit coordination (existence of a perfect one-bit code over a 2^16-value output), an output that does not depend on the secret gives the receiver exactly the blind guess, and a gate that refuses a listed action never lets it execute, whatever signals are used. The first is a witness showing the limits of a cap; the second is nearly definitional; the third holds because the model's only operation is "ask for action a" and the gate filters. The model does not decide which actions count as the malicious one (the `forbidden` list is an input) and it assumes the gate is the only route (`action_gate_complete`, NOT_PROVED), and that zero secret-dependence holds in deployment (`zero_dependence_measured`, NOT_PROVED).

| Lean claim | In plain English (first sentence) | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| `SC05.cap_does_not_prevent` | There exist a sender strategy (for a secret bit, emit a specific one of 65536 possible outputs) and a receiver decoder (read the output and report a bit) such that, for the identity observation of the outputs, the receiver recovers the bit with probability exactly 1. | witness / non-vacuity example |
| `SC05.zero_dependence_is_blind` | If the distribution of the mediated output (given the shared seed) does not depend on the secret at all, then for every seed distribution and every decoder, the probability of recovering a uniform secret equals exactly 1//M/ (the blind guess rate). | general theorem |
| `SC05.forbidden_never_executes` | Consider a system whose only operation is "request action a" and whose state is the list of executed actions; the gate refuses any request whose action id is in the `forbidden` list and otherwise appends it. | general theorem |

## SC-06: Agent plants a covert instruction in shared files, code comments, or other artifacts

**Harm.** An agent's context holds free text written by another agent, or a sensitive action on a value from another agent's artifact runs without a user confirmation of exactly that agent, tool and value.

**In short.** Within a small state machine with a typed canonicalising write path, provenance labels on context entries, user confirmations and an admin halt, `sc06_safe` proves (for every operation sequence from the initial state) that no context entry is marked free text and that every executed sensitive action labelled "from another agent" has a recorded matching confirmation by an id in the users list; `halt_freezes` says a halted state never changes. These properties hold because the model's write path discards the free-text field, labels are computed by the model, and the model's confirm/halt operations check the caller id against lists. The gap to the real harm is large and honest: caller identities are supplied by the (adversarial) operation sequence itself, so credential separation is not modelled (`credential_separation`, NOT_PROVED); every typed field is an unbounded natural number, so "typed" values can still carry an instruction; and the origin label is read off by value, as described below.

## SC-07: Exfiltration of model weights or large sensitive datasets

**Harm.** A sandboxed job sends more than its byte quota off-host, reaches a non-allowlisted destination, or keeps sending after HALT; quantitatively, exfiltrating S information bits through a q-bit per-window quota needs at least ceil(S/q) windows and, with independent per-window catch probability at least p*r and halt on catch, completes before catch with probability at most (1 - p*r)^ceil(S/q).

**In short.** There are three layers. (1) Arithmetic and probability: with a per-window cap q, completing S bits needs at least ceil(S/q) windows with traffic, and a plan that completes survives with probability at most (1-pr)^ceil(S/q) if each traffic window is independently caught with probability at least pr; expected leaked bits are at most q/pr. (2) A small gateway model with allowlist, per-window quota and halt, proved safe, with a progress theorem and a link back to the window count. (3) A forward simulation from a model of the nftables forward chain (including header bytes) into the gateway model, transferring safety. Everything about the real world is left as assumptions: the job's only route is the gateway (`single_route`), no encoding through allowed destinations, timing or covert channels (`adversarial_channels`), under-quota uploads complete (`honest_usefulness`), and that the Python/nftables installation behaves like the Lean concrete model (`model_runtime_correspondence`, tested on one host only). Note several places where the quantities modelled differ from the harm statement: the quota counts the size field of a send only, and the per-window independence and per-window recall are premises.

## SC-08: Unauthorized process or executable runs

**Harm.** A program runs whose loaded-content digest is not on the allowlist, or an interpreter runs a script whose digest is not on the script allowlist (or runs with no script at all).

**In short.** In a model where every exec is decided by a gate that looks up the digest (a function the model treats as given) of the file's current content and consults an allowlist (and, for interpreters, a script allowlist), the gate's record of runs contains only allowlisted programs and, for interpreters, only allowlisted scripts, for every sequence of file writes and exec requests, and nothing changes after an admin halt. This is a proof about the model's own run log. The gap is everything that connects that log to a real machine: that no exec path bypasses the gate, that the hashed bytes are the loaded bytes, that the digest function is collision-resistant (here it is an arbitrary function, with no property assumed), that interpreters are correctly listed as interpreters, and that the halt caller is really an admin.

|---|---|---|

## SC-23 — Prompt-injected document/tool result causes an unauthorized tool action

**Harm.** A sensitive action whose authorization-relevant arguments derive (through label-propagating steps) from tool results executes without a fresh user confirmation of exactly its tool and values.

## SC-24 — Untrusted monitor colludes with generator or learns honeypot phase

**Harm.** A colluding monitor passes real attacks while flagging honeypots, or honeypot-measured recall misstates real recall. The claimed bounds: success at telling real attacks from honeypots is at most (1+TV)/2, and the gap between real and honeypot pass rate is at most TV, where TV is the total variation distance between the view distributions.

**In short.** The four theorems are classical bounds on a monitor whose decision depends only on a stated view of each item: the gap between its pass rates on honeypots and on real attacks is at most TV (`pass_transfer`); its one-shot success at "pass real, flag honeypot" under a 50/50 prior is at most (1+TV)/2 (`collusion_uniform`); a shared-codebook covert message through a canonicalised view of K classes is decoded with probability at most K/|messages| (`canon_channel`); and a monitor learning a hidden phase over m items from bounded per-item leakage guesses correctly with probability at most L^m/2 (`phase_learning`). Everything is conditional on parameters the theorems do not control: TV (which a colluding generator is trying to enlarge, e.g. with a steganographic flag, where TV=1 and the bound is vacuous), the canonicalisation size K, and the per-item leakage; and on the premise that the monitor's decision really depends only on the stated view. These bounds are not a detection guarantee: they say the honeypot measurement transfers up to TV, which is useful only if TV is shown to be small.
