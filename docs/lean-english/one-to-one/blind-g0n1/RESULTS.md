## Corrections to the English

No replacement of a principal claim statement is needed to make the frozen back proposition match the original type. The following replacement sentences resolve ambiguous or overly broad supporting prose.

- **Claim 5, cap caveat:** Replace “It says nothing about spend cap here” with: “The acceptability predicate has no cap clause. The invariant conjunct includes `spent ≤ cap`, so the theorem does carry a spending-cap guarantee through that invariant.” The existing parenthesis partly explains this, but the opening sentence suggests the opposite.
- **Claim 5, append-only wording:** “The final bank extends the initially empty bank. This particular prefix conclusion is vacuous about changes between intermediate states; append-only behavior across individual steps is established by the model's separate prefix theorem.”
- **Claims 8–9, avoided paths:** “An `AvoidedPath` may have zero steps. Each nonzero step follows any listed edge whose target is neither the broker nor the receiver; it does not filter by authority, write, or unknown flags, and it does not constrain the starting vertex.” For claim 9 specifically: “Under edge inclusion and matching broker/receiver IDs, every path defined in this way in the smaller graph is also such a path in the larger graph.”
- **Claim 6, cap reference:** “`Safe t` bounds spending by the cap stored in `t`; every model step preserves that cap, so a trace from `initial cap` gives the same bound against the supplied initial cap.”
- **Claim 10, kind:** “This is a concrete counterexample to downward monotonicity of the acceptance predicate, produced by deleting required database-write edges; it is not evidence that the reduced graph gains a bypass path.” Calling it a weakened-model failure can obscure that the transition model is unchanged.

## Ambiguities exposed

- **Runtime scope and completeness (1, 7).** The word “real” is interpreted through caller-supplied lists and world assumptions. Neither `Faithful` nor `ExtractionFaithful` asserts that the observed list enumerates all real edges. The English's explicit “Does NOT say” qualifications prevent the stronger reading. Even the source comment's word “exhausts” is stronger than the structure's inclusion field; the comparison follows the declaration, not that comment.
- **Permission, identity and rejection (2).** “Legal” is the technical non-gate-credential predicate. It is neither an OS access prohibition nor approval of a business operation. The model's authenticated bank rejects the permitted non-gate direct calls. Binding UID to credential in the real sink remains residual.
- **Approval versus review (3, 7).** “Approved” is the exact-payload identity assertion in SC26. An approver differs from the requester, but may still be another agent. No artifact reviewer or disjoint role lists are inferred. The English's exclusions are respected.
- **Reservation versus effects (4).** Reserved-ID uniqueness and bank-key uniqueness are different properties. The former does not prevent equal payloads under distinct IDs. Cap bounds amount, not the number of arbitrary SC26 effects; only claim 7's unit-cost refinement produces the length bound.
- **Invariant versus acceptability (5).** The cap is absent from `spec.ok` but present in `spec.Inv`. “Says nothing about cap” is inaccurate if read as describing the full theorem. Empty-initial-log prefix and arbitrary-state append-only behavior must also be distinguished.
- **`Safe` versus release provenance (6).** `Safe` is precisely the numerical/nonce/lease predicate. The trace relation has approval/review guards, but a release-provenance statement is not a conjunct of `Safe`. Role separation is a field supplied with `Roles`; witnesses demonstrate model satisfiability, not real identity authenticity.
- **Refinement versus verified execution (7).** The assumed publication equality covers the exact list for the chosen `ops`. Neither a matching history for every real behavior nor correctness of the Python/OS/database follows from the theorem.
- **Path semantics and acceptance (8–10).** Zero-step paths exist; avoidance concerns each step's target, not a global exclusion of every visited vertex. Path-safety is preserved by edge deletion under the stated identity/node conditions, whereas `Accepted` additionally requires positive database writer facts and may fail. The English correctly separates these properties, but should define “avoided.”

## Reproduction and gate integrity

Run from the repository root:

```text
python3 docs/lean-english/one-to-one/blind-g0n1/regate.py
```

The script reads the saved statements/proofs, checks every statement, and invokes the deterministic gate separately for `claim` and `witness` for IDs `bg0n1_1` through `bg0n1_10`. It rewrites only JSON receipts; the helper uses and removes fresh temporary Lean files. It exits nonzero for a missing file, helper mismatch, statement failure, or either proof failure. It needs no old receipts. Imports and project paths come directly from `run_gate_blind.py`.

The local `leangate_imports.py` is a byte-identical copy of the authorized helper; all banned-token and axiom checks are unchanged. `run_gate_blind.diff` records the runner changes: the claim-file glob, `PL_IMPORTS`, and the necessary repository-root depth correction because the output directory is one level deeper. `OUT` still resolves to the runner's own directory. As in the supplied runner, `gate_unmodified` is false because the supplied helper has its documented import-header patch; `security_checks_unchanged` records the byte-for-byte comparison with that authorized helper.

All witnesses are nondegenerate hand-authored model instances, with nonempty authority/path/transaction behavior as appropriate. None is an attestation of observed runtime behavior. Initial failed gate attempts are retained in `FIRST-GATE-FAILURES.json`; they concerned unfolding witness definitions and indentation when constructing a comparison let block, not any repair to a frozen back file.
