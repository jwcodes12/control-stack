# English statements under test

### Claim 1
## `ControlStack.SafetyCaseSC01.sc01_case`

- **Says (plain English):** A conjunction of five separate facts about the SC-01 repair design. (1) Safety, conditional on a side certificate: suppose the receiver sees, besides the five-episode transcript, one extra observation produced by a fixed noisy channel `W` from a side state the sender chooses, and suppose some non-negative function `c` on observations dominates `W` (every `W s o` is at most `c o`) with total mass `sum_o c o` at most 65536/59049 (about 1.11). Then for every shared-seed distribution, every adaptive randomised sender strategy that chooses a (transcript, side state) pair for each 32-bit secret, and every randomised decoder that sees (transcript, side observation), the probability of recovering a uniform 32-bit secret is at most 1/2^16. Here "transcript" is a function from five episode slots to "nothing or one of 8 repair ids" (9 values per slot, 59049 in total). (2) Gate validity: for any true completion probability p between 0 and 9/10, the binomial probability that at least 63 of 64 independent lifetimes complete is at most 1/80. (3) Two arithmetic facts: the upper binomial tail P(X = 64) at p = 0.93382 is at most 1/80, and 0.9 is at most 0.93382. (4) The one-channel, one-slot, eight-value gateway view space has size 9, and the Lean transcript type has exactly 9^5 elements. (5) For the ledger model: on any trace of charge/abort/reopen/corrupt events with no snapshot restore, in which every charge has size 9, starting from a fresh ledger with cap 2^16, each lineage is admitted at most five times.
- **Under these hypotheses:** For (1): the finite type parameters, `rho` is a probability distribution, every `enc omega m` and every `dec omega y` is a probability distribution, and `c` is non-negative, dominates `W` pointwise, and has total mass at most 65536/59049. `W` is not required to be non-negative or normalised; the bound holds for any real function it dominates (so reading it as a probability needs `W` to be a kernel). No condition ties `W` to the VM; that it holds is the assumption `side_kernel` plus `complete_receiver_observations`. For (2): only 0 <= p <= 9/10; "independent lifetimes" is built into the definition of `passProb` as a binomial tail, so independence is an assumption of the model (`independent_lifetimes`), not derived. For (5): no restore events, every charge has size exactly 9, and the lineage label type is arbitrary; that charges really have size 9 and that no rollback happens in the real database is world assumption `ledger_correspondence`.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that a real receiver's observations are limited to the five padded views or to a side kernel with the stated mass; (1) is silent on timing, unmodelled side channels and on any receiver with more than one lineage (the ledger bound is per lineage label, and nothing here prevents obtaining a fresh lineage). (b) Conjunct (3) is only arithmetic about a number; it does NOT say that the experiment actually observed 64 out of 64 completions or that any endpoint was computed from real data (that is recorded outside Lean), and conjunct (2) does not say the honest task actually meets 9/10 on the VM host (`vm_usefulness` is NOT a consequence of this theorem). (c) The five conjuncts are not composed with each other: nothing in the statement derives "view space is 9 per episode, hence transcript is Fin 5 -> 9 values" from the ledger conjunct or from the gateway model; the equality 9^5 is checked on the Lean type, and the match with the actual gateway is by the `ledger_correspondence` and finite-lifetime assumptions.
- **Kind:** general theorem (conjunction; sub-claims (3) and (4) are finite computations, (4) is `decide`/`norm_num`)

### Claim 2
## `ControlStack.GatewayCore.core_bound`

- **Says (plain English):** Model the gateway as a state machine over raw events (send a raw payload on a channel or on an unknown channel, any other side action, close). A validator `valid` accepts or rejects each payload; a rejected payload, unknown channel, side action or slot overflow "blanks" the episode so the delivered view is all NULL; otherwise accepted values are appended to the channel; at close (or at the end of the trace) the receiver is delivered, per channel, its accepted values padded with NULL up to the slot count. The theorem: let the sender choose, for each shared-seed value and each secret in M, any randomised trace of exactly L raw events, and let the decoder see only the final delivered view. Then the probability that the decoder outputs the secret (averaged over the seed and over a uniform secret) is at most `viewSpace / |M|`, where `viewSpace` is the product over channels of the sum, for j from 0 to the slot count, of (size of that channel's value set)^j.
- **Under these hypotheses:** The seed distribution, every encoder row and every decoder row are probability distributions; M, the seed type, the raw payload type, the event type, and each channel's value type are finite (the event type's finiteness is a separate instance argument); M is non-empty. `valid` is arbitrary (the bound does not depend on it). The decoder is given only the final view (not timing, trace length, or anything else); that the real receiver sees only this view is a world assumption not made by the theorem.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not a bound on a multi-episode lifetime; it bounds one gateway view space. The five-episode bound comes from multiplying five such spaces elsewhere. (b) Not that the Python gateway implements these transitions (the file docstring says it matches `gateway/gateway.py`, but there is no refinement proof here). (c) The bound is a counting bound (few possible views); it does not say that the view carries little useful information, only that it cannot identify a uniform secret except with probability at most `viewSpace/|M|`.
- **Kind:** general theorem

### Claim 3
## `ControlStack.Covert.covert_bound_lifetime`

- **Says (plain English):** Suppose the receiver's entire observation over the secret's lifetime is a tuple of E episode views, where episode e's view takes values in a finite set `V e`. Then for any shared-seed distribution, any adaptive randomised encoder from secrets to whole view-tuples, and any randomised decoder from view-tuples to secrets, the probability that the decoder outputs the uniform secret is at most (product over episodes of |V e|) divided by |M|.
- **Under these hypotheses:** finite types; M non-empty; seed distribution, encoder rows and decoder rows are probability distributions. Implicitly (from the types) the receiver observes exactly the tuple and nothing else, and the secret is uniform and independent of the seed and of the receiver's inputs (the file header lists these as deployment assumptions "not this theorem").
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that the real lifetime observation is such a tuple (no timing, no side channels, fixed number of episodes). (b) Not that the product is small: the smallness (at most 2^16) comes from the ledger enforcing a cap, which is a separate theorem; this one is just the product form. (c) The encoder is allowed to see the full tuple structure in one shot (a joint law over tuples), which is no weaker for the sender than adaptivity across episodes; but this is exactly why the bound is in terms of the whole tuple and not per-episode.
- **Kind:** general theorem (essentially the one-shot counting bound `covert_bound` with |V| = product of cardinalities)

### Claim 4
## `ControlStack.ChannelInstances.sc02_accumulated`

- **Says (plain English):** Consider E rounds. In each round the sender (who knows the secret and a shared seed, and may keep arbitrary hidden state, and may adapt to everything so far) produces a hidden state value and a visible output; the receiver sees only the visible outputs and finally guesses the secret using a randomised decoder. Suppose there is a certificate `c`, a function of the visible history so far and the next visible output, which (after summing out the hidden state) bounds the probability weight of each visible output, for every secret and every seed, and suppose that for every visible history of length i the total certificate mass over next outputs is at most `L i`. Then the probability of recovering a uniform k-bit secret is at most (product over rounds e below E of `L e`) divided by 2^k.
- **Under these hypotheses:** The seed distribution and every decoder row are probability distributions; kernel entries are non-negative (they need not sum to 1, so the kernel may lose mass, which only lowers the success value); the visible alphabet, hidden alphabet and seed type are finite; `c` is non-negative; `L` is non-negative. The domination hypothesis (for every secret m, every seed, every history, the total weight of visible output `o` summed over hidden states is at most `c(history) o`) and the per-history mass bound are the model conditions that encode "each episode's view is dominated by a certificate of mass L_e". Whether a real episode has such a certificate is a measurement or enforcement obligation (world assumption `episode_certificates`). The certificate does not depend on the secret, which is the content of the domination premise. "Episode e" is round e (the history length).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that any real episode has the stated mass; Lean takes `L` and `c` as inputs. (b) Not a bound on anything except exact recovery of the k-bit secret by the decoder; it says nothing about partial recovery or recovery of other functions of the secret. (c) The decoder sees visible outputs only: any observation channel outside the modelled visible outputs (timing, shared caches visible to the receiver, anything not in `O`) is not covered.
- **Kind:** general theorem (direct instance of `Leakage.covert_seq` plus `mass_le_prod`)

### Claim 5
## `ControlStack.ChannelInstances.rekey_epoch`

- **Says (plain English):** Same setting as above, but with a total of n rounds, and a window of E rounds starting at round a (requiring a + E at most n). Suppose the certificate has total mass at most L at every history whose length lies inside the window, and at most 1 at every history whose length lies outside it. Then the probability of recovering the (uniform) secret `m` is at most L^E / |M|, whatever happens in the other rounds, and even though the decoder sees all n rounds.
- **Under these hypotheses:** Same finite-type, distribution and non-negativity conditions as `sc02_accumulated`; domination of the kernel by the certificate for every secret and seed; `L >= 0`; `hin` (in-window mass at most L); `hout` (out-of-window mass at most 1). The "secret is fresh for this epoch" modelling (other secrets folded into the seed) is a modelling convention in the docstring, not a Lean hypothesis. The independence premise "no persistent state carries this epoch's secret into other rounds" is not stated separately; it is encoded only as `hout`, because a mass-1 m-independent-style certificate cannot dominate outputs that reveal `m` (if two secrets produce different outputs with certainty, the certificate would need mass at least 2).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that re-keying works in a deployment: `hout` must be established for the real system (this is `rekey_independence`, NOT_PROVED); if an earlier epoch's cache carries the new secret into later rounds, the premise fails. (b) Not a bound on recovering earlier or later epochs' secrets simultaneously. (c) The statement does not require L to be at least 1; if a caller supplies L below 1 for a window in which the sender really can emit a normalised kernel, the domination hypothesis simply cannot be met, so the theorem would be vacuous for that choice rather than giving a better-than-blind bound.
- **Kind:** general theorem

### Claim 6
## `ControlStack.ChannelInstances.log_channel`

- **Says (plain English):** Each of m rounds produces a hidden sender state and a visible log record that is one of `Kt` templates together with `f` numeric fields each quantised to one of `Q` levels (so each record is one of Kt * Q^f possible values). The sender knows the secret and a shared seed and may adapt to the entire history. The receiver sees the sequence of visible records and then guesses the secret with a randomised decoder. Then the probability that the guess equals a uniform secret is at most Kt^m * Q^(f*m) / |M|.
- **Under these hypotheses:** finite types; M non-empty; seed distribution and decoder rows are probability distributions; sender kernel entries are non-negative and, for every secret and history, sum to at most 1 over (hidden state, visible record) (sub-probability kernel: probability mass may be lost, e.g. by emitting nothing, which in this model simply gives that path zero success). Exactly m rounds are modelled. That the real visible output is one such record (canonicalisation is complete, and no timing/ordering/length information escapes) is the world assumption.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that real logs are canonicalised; if a free-text field exists the bound is void (the neighbouring `free_text_leaks` shows probability 1 from one record). (b) Not about variable-length logs: the number of rounds m is fixed, and a sub-probability kernel "emitting nothing" is penalised in the payoff rather than modelled as visible information, so the count of records or an absence of a record as a signal is not covered. (c) Not about timing or error-string channels, which must be inside the canonicalised view or certified separately.
- **Kind:** general theorem (instance of `covert_alphabet` plus cardinality arithmetic)

### Claim 7
## `ControlStack.ChannelInstances.canonical_seq`

- **Says (plain English):** If each of m items reaches the monitor only as one of `Kc` canonical classes, then any adaptive generator (with hidden state and a shared seed) and any decoder recover a uniform message with probability at most Kc^m / |M|.
- **Under these hypotheses:** the same type of hypotheses as `log_channel` (finite types, M non-empty, distributions for seed and decoder, non-negative sub-probability kernel). Exactly m rounds.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that a canonicaliser with Kc classes exists or is trusted. (b) Not a bound for one-shot or unbounded-length use; it is for exactly m items. (c) Not a statement about meaning: a class can encode a bit of the secret; the bound is that it cannot encode more than m log2 Kc bits.
- **Kind:** general theorem (restatement of `covert_alphabet` with `O = Fin Kc`)

### Claim 8
## `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.windows_needed`

- **Says (plain English):** If a plan sends a list of per-window bit counts, each at most q (q positive), and the plan's total is at least S, then the number of windows in which any traffic is sent is at least ceil(S/q).
- **Under these hypotheses:** `q > 0`; every per-window count is at most q (the quota enforced); the total is at least S. The numbers are abstract bit counts, so that they are information bits of the secret (rather than bytes or compressible content) is a modelling premise.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not a statement about time or windows being real wall-clock intervals. (b) Not that a real gateway caps each window at q (that is the quota's job; here it is a hypothesis). (c) Not that the secret needs only S bits: if S is measured in raw bytes and the attacker can compress, the bound must be read with the compressed size.
- **Kind:** general theorem (pigeonhole arithmetic)

### Claim 9
## `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.sc07_mixed`

- **Says (plain English):** Take a random choice of plan (weights `w` over a finite set of plans, each plan a list of per-window bit counts) which is made without any information about the audits. Cap each plan at q bits per window. For each plan define its survival value as the product, over its traffic windows i, of (1 - c i), where c i is the probability that window i's traffic is caught. The weighted sum, over plans whose capped total is at least S, of weight times survival value, is at most (1 - pr)^ceil(S/q), provided every c i lies between pr and 1.
- **Under these hypotheses:** `q > 0`; `0 <= pr <= 1`; each per-window catch probability `c i` is at least `pr` and at most 1; `w` is a probability distribution. The catch probabilities being independent across windows is built into the definition of "survive" (a product), so independence is a premise. That every traffic window, however few bits it carries, is caught with probability at least p*r (audited with probability at least p and detection recall at least r), and that a catch halts the job, are world assumptions. Plans are fixed in advance (the attacker gets no audit information beyond being caught).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) The quantity bounded is "the whole plan is never caught, and the capped total reaches S", not literally "S bits have left before the first catch": a plan that keeps sending after reaching S has to survive extra windows, so the Lean quantity is for the whole plan; the relation to "completes before catch" needs an unproved-in-Lean truncation step (stop at the first window that reaches S). (b) Not a bound on a hidden fixed budget or adaptive attacker (that is `sc07_fixed_budget`). (c) Not about covert, timing or destination-choice channels.
- **Kind:** general theorem

### Claim 10
## `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.sc07_fixed_budget`

- **Says (plain English):** Suppose exactly B of N windows are chosen uniformly at random as audited, hidden from the attacker, with each audit of a traffic window missing the traffic with probability at most 1 - r given everything the attacker has done and seen. The attacker is adaptive: each window it decides whether to send traffic according to a rule depending on its visible history (traffic decisions and catch flags), and it may keep hidden state. Then the probability (averaged over the audit schedule and the attacker's randomness) that it sends traffic in at least ceil(S/q) windows and is never caught is at most `hypMiss N B ceil(S/q) r`, the hypergeometric miss probability with imperfect recall r.
- **Under these hypotheses:** `B <= N`; `0 <= r <= 1`; for every schedule of size B, the attacker's kernel is non-negative, sums to at most 1 per history, and its probability of a non-caught output with traffic decision `a` is at most (the attacker's own traffic law at that history) times (1 - r if the current window is audited and a is traffic, else 1). That last premise is the recall premise, which must hold per history, not on average (the file header says a population-average detection rate is not enough); the traffic law is independent of the schedule, so the attacker learns nothing about the schedule except catch flags. The numbers S and q enter only through k = ceil(S/q); the statement itself contains no byte or quota condition.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) The connection "completing exfiltration needs at least k traffic windows" is `windows_needed`, which is not part of this statement; the bounded event is "at least k traffic windows and never caught" over exactly N windows. (b) Not that any deployment hides the schedule or achieves recall r per history. (c) It does not apply when the schedule leaks (observable schedule: see `observable_schedule_exfil`).
- **Kind:** refinement/bridge (instance of `ReservedRecall.adaptive_recall_bound`)
