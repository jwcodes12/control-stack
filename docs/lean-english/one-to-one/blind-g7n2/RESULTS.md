Every `Claim` includes gate-proved `BackFull ↔ BackSchema` and `OrigFull ↔ OrigSchema` encoding checks. The remaining conjunct is the pointwise comparison, freshness/cost normalization where needed, or claim 6's explicitly diagnostic counterexamples. Comparing arbitrary output states in claims 5, 9–11 is stronger than comparing only reachable outputs. For claim 9 the proof is equality substitution for arbitrary observations, not evaluation of an iff between true closed propositions. No original safety theorem supplies either side of a comparison.

Claim 5's type difference requires explanation. For a list `U`, the set interpretation is `{u | u ∈ U}`; list order and duplicates do not affect membership. Under disjointness, every admin lies outside the adversary membership predicate, including when that predicate describes an infinite set. The gated result holds for independently chosen disjoint list and set predicates and every possible output state: both provenance conclusions require an admin, so changing this representation cannot change their truth. It does not assert that all sets are finite.

Claim 6's gated diagnostic has two explicit states. An unchecked state can have lease budget 100, assignment `(1,0)` and lease-0 total 5 while recording worker **2**, and a checked state can contain a **zero-unit entry** while having total zero. These satisfy the back observation predicate but fail the original's exact logs. These are predicate counterexamples only. The separately gated witness checks the actual forged trace's exact logs and its unchecked budget and assignment. ## Ambiguities exposed

- **Closed computation versus observation predicate (claim 6).** “Five units” can mean a per-lease sum, a total sum, or an exact singleton log; “nothing consumed” can mean total zero or an empty log. Zero-unit accepted steps distinguish the latter two meanings. Additional lease/assignment/subtrace facts are true in the fixed computation but are not the original conclusion. The supplied equivalence restriction prevents solving this mismatch by merely evaluating two closed true propositions.
- **Admissibility (claim 8).** Ordinary usage suggests all acceptance conditions, but the actual hypotheses are assignment, existing budget and capacity. An initially halted or revoked state is permitted and is covered by an escape clause. The English's explicitly listed hypotheses support the back reading, but the opening word should change.
- **Charge versus report (claim 7).** Honest reporting is `reported = actual`; it is not `reported = charge`. The minimum-one charge also bounds the number of accepted zero-unit steps.
- **Adversary-set representation (claim 5).** The back quantifies over sets, the original over finite lists. This is harmless for the compared conclusions under disjointness, but should be disclosed rather than silently treating the types as identical.
- **World assumptions versus formal hypotheses (claims 1, 10–13).** Trusted identity, meter coverage, kernel enforcement, atomic kill and absence of escape are interpretation/model premises, not separately proved facts about a deployed kernel. The English mostly distinguishes these correctly. No external runtime evidence was read or validated in this run.
- **Concrete safety predicate (claim 11).** The original is `SC28.Good` of `α`; the back spells out counters. Their equivalence is definitional bookkeeping, which does not strengthen the result to cover unplaced consumption.
