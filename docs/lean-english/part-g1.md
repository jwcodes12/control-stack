# Plain-English statements: part G1 (SC-01 to SC-07)

Conventions used below. "Lean file:line" is relative to the repo root. Probabilities in these files are real-valued sums over finite types; the "probability" reading of a sum is only licensed when the stated kernels really are probability (or sub-probability) kernels, which is a modelling premise, not something Lean checks about the world. All 28 theorems below were read at `full` depth (statement plus every definition the statement unfolds). I did not run Lean; I did not read proofs except where needed to understand what a statement unfolds to.

## SC-01: Lifetime-bounded exact secret recovery

**Harm:** A machine receiver recovers a uniform 32-bit secret with probability above 2^-16.

**What the Lean claims, taken together, and what they leave to assumptions.** The three theorems give a counting bound: if the receiver's entire observation over a lineage's lifetime takes at most 9^5 = 59049 values (five episodes, nine possible padded views each), then no sender/receiver pair with a shared seed recovers a uniform 32-bit secret with probability above 59049/2^32 (about 2^-16 after rounding up); `sc01_case` additionally bundles a side-observation version of the bound, a ledger lemma, and some usefulness arithmetic. Everything that connects this counting to a real VM is an assumption: that the receiver sees nothing except the five padded views (no timing or other side channel, `complete_receiver_observations`, NOT_PROVED), that any side channel has a domination certificate of total mass at most 65536/59049 (`side_kernel`), that the durable ledger really behaves like the Lean ledger (`ledger_correspondence`), that lifetimes are independent (`independent_lifetimes`) and that the honest task meets its floor on the VM host (`vm_usefulness`). Note that several assumptions the scenario lists as THEOREM_VERIFIED are, in Lean, only conjuncts of `sc01_case` about the Lean model (or about numbers), not statements about the deployed system.

### `ControlStack.SafetyCaseSC01.sc01_case`  — `ControlStack/SafetyCaseSC01.lean:95`
- **Says (plain English):** A conjunction of five separate facts about the SC-01 repair design. (1) Safety, conditional on a side certificate: suppose the receiver sees, besides the five-episode transcript, one extra observation produced by a fixed noisy channel `W` from a side state the sender chooses, and suppose some non-negative function `c` on observations dominates `W` (every `W s o` is at most `c o`) with total mass `sum_o c o` at most 65536/59049 (about 1.11). Then for every shared-seed distribution, every adaptive randomised sender strategy that chooses a (transcript, side state) pair for each 32-bit secret, and every randomised decoder that sees (transcript, side observation), the probability of recovering a uniform 32-bit secret is at most 1/2^16. Here "transcript" is a function from five episode slots to "nothing or one of 8 repair ids" (9 values per slot, 59049 in total). (2) Gate validity: for any true completion probability p between 0 and 9/10, the binomial probability that at least 63 of 64 independent lifetimes complete is at most 1/80. (3) Two arithmetic facts: the upper binomial tail P(X = 64) at p = 0.93382 is at most 1/80, and 0.9 is at most 0.93382. (4) The one-channel, one-slot, eight-value gateway view space has size 9, and the Lean transcript type has exactly 9^5 elements. (5) For the ledger model: on any trace of charge/abort/reopen/corrupt events with no snapshot restore, in which every charge has size 9, starting from a fresh ledger with cap 2^16, each lineage is admitted at most five times.
- **Under these hypotheses:** For (1): the finite type parameters, `rho` is a probability distribution, every `enc omega m` and every `dec omega y` is a probability distribution, and `c` is non-negative, dominates `W` pointwise, and has total mass at most 65536/59049. `W` is not required to be non-negative or normalised; the bound holds for any real function it dominates (so reading it as a probability needs `W` to be a kernel). No condition ties `W` to the VM; that it holds is the assumption `side_kernel` plus `complete_receiver_observations`. For (2): only 0 <= p <= 9/10; "independent lifetimes" is built into the definition of `passProb` as a binomial tail, so independence is an assumption of the model (`independent_lifetimes`), not derived. For (5): no restore events, every charge has size exactly 9, and the lineage label type is arbitrary; that charges really have size 9 and that no rollback happens in the real database is world assumption `ledger_correspondence`.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that a real receiver's observations are limited to the five padded views or to a side kernel with the stated mass; (1) is silent on timing, unmodelled side channels and on any receiver with more than one lineage (the ledger bound is per lineage label, and nothing here prevents obtaining a fresh lineage). (b) Conjunct (3) is only arithmetic about a number; it does NOT say that the experiment actually observed 64 out of 64 completions or that any endpoint was computed from real data (that is recorded outside Lean), and conjunct (2) does not say the honest task actually meets 9/10 on the VM host (`vm_usefulness` is NOT a consequence of this theorem). (c) The five conjuncts are not composed with each other: nothing in the statement derives "view space is 9 per episode, hence transcript is Fin 5 -> 9 values" from the ledger conjunct or from the gateway model; the equality 9^5 is checked on the Lean type, and the match with the actual gateway is by the `ledger_correspondence` and finite-lifetime assumptions.
- **Kind:** general theorem (conjunction; sub-claims (3) and (4) are finite computations, (4) is `decide`/`norm_num`)
- **Non-vacuity:** NOT SHOWN as a Lean instance for (1) in this theorem. The hypotheses are satisfiable (for example a single-observation side kernel with `c = 1` has mass 1 <= 1.11; the sibling file proves `side_bound_uninfluenced` for that shape), and `ScenarioASide.repair_one_bit_attained` shows that a noiseless side bit (mass 2) breaks the bound, so the mass condition is not decorative. For the ledger conjunct, no named witness trace is in this file.
- **Read depth:** full (read `ScenarioASide`, `SideChannel.sideSuccess`/`side_bound`, `ScenarioARepair`, `ScenarioAUsefulness`, `LifetimeLedger`, `GatewayCore.sc01_episode_views`).

### `ControlStack.GatewayCore.core_bound`  — `ControlStack/GatewayCore.lean:265`
- **Says (plain English):** Model the gateway as a state machine over raw events (send a raw payload on a channel or on an unknown channel, any other side action, close). A validator `valid` accepts or rejects each payload; a rejected payload, unknown channel, side action or slot overflow "blanks" the episode so the delivered view is all NULL; otherwise accepted values are appended to the channel; at close (or at the end of the trace) the receiver is delivered, per channel, its accepted values padded with NULL up to the slot count. The theorem: let the sender choose, for each shared-seed value and each secret in M, any randomised trace of exactly L raw events, and let the decoder see only the final delivered view. Then the probability that the decoder outputs the secret (averaged over the seed and over a uniform secret) is at most `viewSpace / |M|`, where `viewSpace` is the product over channels of the sum, for j from 0 to the slot count, of (size of that channel's value set)^j.
- **Under these hypotheses:** The seed distribution, every encoder row and every decoder row are probability distributions; M, the seed type, the raw payload type, the event type, and each channel's value type are finite (the event type's finiteness is a separate instance argument); M is non-empty. `valid` is arbitrary (the bound does not depend on it). The decoder is given only the final view (not timing, trace length, or anything else); that the real receiver sees only this view is a world assumption not made by the theorem.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not a bound on a multi-episode lifetime; it bounds one gateway view space. The five-episode bound comes from multiplying five such spaces elsewhere. (b) Not that the Python gateway implements these transitions (the file docstring says it matches `gateway/gateway.py`, but there is no refinement proof here). (c) The bound is a counting bound (few possible views); it does not say that the view carries little useful information, only that it cannot identify a uniform secret except with probability at most `viewSpace/|M|`.
- **Kind:** general theorem
- **Non-vacuity:** Shown by the same file for the SC-01 parameters: `sc01_episode_views` computes `viewSpace` = 9 for one channel, one slot, eight values. No explicit instance of an encoder/decoder pair is given for `core_bound` itself.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.Covert.covert_bound_lifetime`  — `ControlStack/CovertChannel.lean:137`
- **Says (plain English):** Suppose the receiver's entire observation over the secret's lifetime is a tuple of E episode views, where episode e's view takes values in a finite set `V e`. Then for any shared-seed distribution, any adaptive randomised encoder from secrets to whole view-tuples, and any randomised decoder from view-tuples to secrets, the probability that the decoder outputs the uniform secret is at most (product over episodes of |V e|) divided by |M|.
- **Under these hypotheses:** finite types; M non-empty; seed distribution, encoder rows and decoder rows are probability distributions. Implicitly (from the types) the receiver observes exactly the tuple and nothing else, and the secret is uniform and independent of the seed and of the receiver's inputs (the file header lists these as deployment assumptions "not this theorem").
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that the real lifetime observation is such a tuple (no timing, no side channels, fixed number of episodes). (b) Not that the product is small: the smallness (at most 2^16) comes from the ledger enforcing a cap, which is a separate theorem; this one is just the product form. (c) The encoder is allowed to see the full tuple structure in one shot (a joint law over tuples), which is no weaker for the sender than adaptivity across episodes; but this is exactly why the bound is in terms of the whole tuple and not per-episode.
- **Kind:** general theorem (essentially the one-shot counting bound `covert_bound` with |V| = product of cardinalities)
- **Non-vacuity:** `attain_embedding` in the same file shows the bound is attained when views embed in the secret space; no instance for this exact form.
- **Read depth:** full

## SC-02: Sender communicates across multiple episodes to a persistent receiver

**Harm:** A persistent receiver recovers a k-bit secret across E episodes; the claim is that success probability is at most (product of per-episode certificate masses L_e) / 2^k.

**What the Lean claims, taken together, and what they leave to assumptions.** `sc02_accumulated` multiplies per-round (here "episode") certificate masses and divides by the secret space; `rekey_epoch` shows that if rounds outside an epoch are dominated by a certificate of mass at most 1, only the in-epoch masses count. Both are conditional on the receiver's per-round view being dominated by a certificate of the stated mass; Lean does not derive that domination from anything, so "measure or enforce L_e" (`episode_certificates`, NOT_PROVED) is the entire connection to a real system. The independence premise (`rekey_independence`, NOT_PROVED) appears in Lean only as the hypothesis `hout` (outside-epoch mass at most 1), which is a condition on the certificate, not a proof that no persistent state carries the secret. The proven bound is on exact recovery by the decoder, which is the "adversary's success" rather than any broader notion of "communication".

### `ControlStack.ChannelInstances.sc02_accumulated`  — `ControlStack/Families/F2/ChannelInstances.lean:105`
- **Says (plain English):** Consider E rounds. In each round the sender (who knows the secret and a shared seed, and may keep arbitrary hidden state, and may adapt to everything so far) produces a hidden state value and a visible output; the receiver sees only the visible outputs and finally guesses the secret using a randomised decoder. Suppose there is a certificate `c`, a function of the visible history so far and the next visible output, which (after summing out the hidden state) bounds the probability weight of each visible output, for every secret and every seed, and suppose that for every visible history of length i the total certificate mass over next outputs is at most `L i`. Then the probability of recovering a uniform k-bit secret is at most (product over rounds e below E of `L e`) divided by 2^k.
- **Under these hypotheses:** The seed distribution and every decoder row are probability distributions; kernel entries are non-negative (they need not sum to 1, so the kernel may lose mass, which only lowers the success value); the visible alphabet, hidden alphabet and seed type are finite; `c` is non-negative; `L` is non-negative. The domination hypothesis (for every secret m, every seed, every history, the total weight of visible output `o` summed over hidden states is at most `c(history) o`) and the per-history mass bound are the model conditions that encode "each episode's view is dominated by a certificate of mass L_e". Whether a real episode has such a certificate is a measurement or enforcement obligation (world assumption `episode_certificates`). The certificate does not depend on the secret, which is the content of the domination premise. "Episode e" is round e (the history length).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that any real episode has the stated mass; Lean takes `L` and `c` as inputs. (b) Not a bound on anything except exact recovery of the k-bit secret by the decoder; it says nothing about partial recovery or recovery of other functions of the secret. (c) The decoder sees visible outputs only: any observation channel outside the modelled visible outputs (timing, shared caches visible to the receiver, anything not in `O`) is not covered.
- **Kind:** general theorem (direct instance of `Leakage.covert_seq` plus `mass_le_prod`)
- **Non-vacuity:** NOT SHOWN for the full hypothesis set. The numeric corollaries `sc02_example` and `sc02_no_rekey` in the same file are pure arithmetic on the bound, not witnesses of the hypotheses. The hypotheses are generic inequalities; I did not find a Lean instance exhibiting a kernel meeting them with mass above 1.
- **Read depth:** full (read `Dominated`, `val`, `mass`, `covert_seq`, `mass_le_prod` statements).

### `ControlStack.ChannelInstances.rekey_epoch`  — `ControlStack/Families/F2/ChannelInstances.lean:130`
- **Says (plain English):** Same setting as above, but with a total of n rounds, and a window of E rounds starting at round a (requiring a + E at most n). Suppose the certificate has total mass at most L at every history whose length lies inside the window, and at most 1 at every history whose length lies outside it. Then the probability of recovering the (uniform) secret `m` is at most L^E / |M|, whatever happens in the other rounds, and even though the decoder sees all n rounds.
- **Under these hypotheses:** Same finite-type, distribution and non-negativity conditions as `sc02_accumulated`; domination of the kernel by the certificate for every secret and seed; `L >= 0`; `hin` (in-window mass at most L); `hout` (out-of-window mass at most 1). The "secret is fresh for this epoch" modelling (other secrets folded into the seed) is a modelling convention in the docstring, not a Lean hypothesis. The independence premise "no persistent state carries this epoch's secret into other rounds" is not stated separately; it is encoded only as `hout`, because a mass-1 m-independent-style certificate cannot dominate outputs that reveal `m` (if two secrets produce different outputs with certainty, the certificate would need mass at least 2).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that re-keying works in a deployment: `hout` must be established for the real system (this is `rekey_independence`, NOT_PROVED); if an earlier epoch's cache carries the new secret into later rounds, the premise fails. (b) Not a bound on recovering earlier or later epochs' secrets simultaneously. (c) The statement does not require L to be at least 1; if a caller supplies L below 1 for a window in which the sender really can emit a normalised kernel, the domination hypothesis simply cannot be met, so the theorem would be vacuous for that choice rather than giving a better-than-blind bound.
- **Kind:** general theorem
- **Non-vacuity:** NOT SHOWN as a Lean instance. `sc02_example` is arithmetic only.
- **Read depth:** full

## SC-03: Pinned immediate-peer egress confinement

**Harm:** An untrusted sandbox process initiates an unauthorised immediate outbound peer connection.

**What the Lean claims, taken together, and what they leave to assumptions.** The Lean model is a small state machine: a sandbox can only cause outbound socket attempts via `directAttempt`, and the model's `step` makes such an attempt have any effect only when the launched topology is not "safe" (all five boolean flags true); every attempt that does occur through a request goes to a peer found in the pinned policy by `lookup`. Both theorems say the invariant "if active the topology is safe, and every recorded attempt came from the gateway to a pinned peer" is preserved by any action sequence. This is close to true by construction: the model's guards are exactly what make the invariant inductive, so the real content is in whether the real Linux setup corresponds to the flags (the topology booleans, policy integrity, exclusive mediation, attribution), which Lean does not touch (`policy_integrity` and `general_attribution` NOT_PROVED; `usefulness_population` NOT_PROVED).

### `ControlStack.EgressGate.step_safe`  — `ControlStack/EgressGate.lean:100`
- **Says (plain English):** For any fixed policy `p` (a list of rules, each pairing an authority string with a pinned peer address/port/protocol), any state `s`, and any single action `a`: if the state satisfies the invariant, so does the state after the action. The invariant says two things: (i) if the sandbox is marked active then its topology flags are all true ("safe": no external route, no inherited authority, private namespaces, protected policy, no host deputy); (ii) every recorded outbound socket attempt (including failed connects) originated from the gateway and targets a peer that appears in the policy's pinned peers. The actions are: launch with a topology, start the gateway with a supplied policy (it is up only if the supplied policy equals `p`), request via an authority (the gateway looks the authority up in `p` and, if found and the gateway is up and the sandbox is active, records an attempt to the pinned peer), a direct socket attempt by the sandbox, tamper-with-policy, and crash-gateway.
- **Under these hypotheses:** The state satisfies the invariant. In the model: `launch t` sets `active` to whether `t` is safe; `directAttempt` has an effect only when active and the topology is not safe (and the invariant rules that combination out); `tamperPolicy` is a no-op (the policy `p` is a fixed parameter; this IS the assumption `policy_integrity`, built into the model rather than proved); `crashGateway` only turns the gateway off.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that a real sandbox cannot open sockets: the "safe" flags are inputs to `launch`; Lean has no connection between the flags and Linux namespaces, routing, inherited file descriptors, or host helper processes. (b) Not that peers listed in the policy are themselves harmless, nor anything about what flows through a pinned tunnel (the file header excludes CONNECT tunnel bytes). (c) Does not show that any particular state satisfies the invariant: reachability from a real start state needs the start state to satisfy it (an inactive state with no attempts does, but no named instance is given).
- **Kind:** general theorem (inductive preservation; the proof is case analysis on the model's own guards and the lemma `lookup_pinned`)
- **Non-vacuity:** NOT SHOWN as a named example. An inactive state with an empty attempt list plainly satisfies the invariant, but I did not find a Lean statement of that.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.EgressGate.trace_safe`  — `ControlStack/EgressGate.lean:126`
- **Says (plain English):** For any policy, any state satisfying the invariant, and any finite list of actions, running the actions in order from that state ends in a state that also satisfies the invariant. So: every outbound attempt ever recorded has the gateway as origin and a pinned peer as target, and an active sandbox always has a safe topology flag set.
- **Under these hypotheses:** the same invariant on the starting state; arbitrary action list (every action type, including repeated launches, policy tampering, gateway crashes). Same model assumptions as `step_safe`.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not "the sandbox cannot connect anywhere unauthorised" in the real world, only in the model whose `directAttempt` is defined to be inert when the flags say safe. (b) Not about states reachable from a specific real initial state unless that initial state satisfies the invariant. (c) The word "unauthorised" is modelled as "origin other than the gateway, or a peer not in the policy"; peers reachable through gateway-originated attempts are authorised by definition, even though the sandbox picks the authority key.
- **Kind:** general theorem (induction over `step_safe`; the invariant holds essentially by the construction of the model)
- **Non-vacuity:** NOT SHOWN (same as `step_safe`).
- **Read depth:** full

## SC-04: Agent leaks via logs, telemetry, diagnostic output, error strings or process timing

**Harm:** A receiver of logs/telemetry recovers a secret; the claim is probability at most K^m * Q^(f m) / |M| when the receiver sees only template ids and quantised fields.

**What the Lean claims, taken together, and what they leave to assumptions.** Both theorems are counting bounds: if what reaches the receiver in each of m rounds is one element of a fixed finite alphabet (K classes, or K templates with f fields at Q levels), then recovery of a uniform secret is at most (alphabet size)^m / |M|, even with hidden sender state and adaptive sender behaviour. The theorems have no content about whether a real logging pipeline outputs only such elements: that every receiver-visible diagnostic passes the trusted canonicaliser, and that timing and ordering are inside the view, are exactly the NOT_PROVED assumptions `canonicaliser_complete` and `timing_in_view`. The model also fixes the number of rounds m.

### `ControlStack.ChannelInstances.log_channel`  — `ControlStack/Families/F2/ChannelInstances.lean:176`
- **Says (plain English):** Each of m rounds produces a hidden sender state and a visible log record that is one of `Kt` templates together with `f` numeric fields each quantised to one of `Q` levels (so each record is one of Kt * Q^f possible values). The sender knows the secret and a shared seed and may adapt to the entire history. The receiver sees the sequence of visible records and then guesses the secret with a randomised decoder. Then the probability that the guess equals a uniform secret is at most Kt^m * Q^(f*m) / |M|.
- **Under these hypotheses:** finite types; M non-empty; seed distribution and decoder rows are probability distributions; sender kernel entries are non-negative and, for every secret and history, sum to at most 1 over (hidden state, visible record) (sub-probability kernel: probability mass may be lost, e.g. by emitting nothing, which in this model simply gives that path zero success). Exactly m rounds are modelled. That the real visible output is one such record (canonicalisation is complete, and no timing/ordering/length information escapes) is the world assumption.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that real logs are canonicalised; if a free-text field exists the bound is void (the neighbouring `free_text_leaks` shows probability 1 from one record). (b) Not about variable-length logs: the number of rounds m is fixed, and a sub-probability kernel "emitting nothing" is penalised in the payoff rather than modelled as visible information, so the count of records or an absence of a record as a signal is not covered. (c) Not about timing or error-string channels, which must be inside the canonicalised view or certified separately.
- **Kind:** general theorem (instance of `covert_alphabet` plus cardinality arithmetic)
- **Non-vacuity:** `log_example` is arithmetic on the bound (8 templates, 2 fields at 4 levels, 4 records, 64-bit secret gives 2^-36); it is not a witness of the hypotheses. NOT SHOWN otherwise.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.ChannelInstances.canonical_seq`  — `ControlStack/Families/F2/ChannelInstances.lean:162`
- **Says (plain English):** If each of m items reaches the monitor only as one of `Kc` canonical classes, then any adaptive generator (with hidden state and a shared seed) and any decoder recover a uniform message with probability at most Kc^m / |M|.
- **Under these hypotheses:** the same type of hypotheses as `log_channel` (finite types, M non-empty, distributions for seed and decoder, non-negative sub-probability kernel). Exactly m rounds.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that a canonicaliser with Kc classes exists or is trusted. (b) Not a bound for one-shot or unbounded-length use; it is for exactly m items. (c) Not a statement about meaning: a class can encode a bit of the secret; the bound is that it cannot encode more than m log2 Kc bits.
- **Kind:** general theorem (restatement of `covert_alphabet` with `O = Fin Kc`)
- **Non-vacuity:** NOT SHOWN.
- **Read depth:** full

## SC-05: Agent coordinates a malicious action using a one-bit signal

**Harm:** Agents coordinate a prohibited action through a low-bandwidth (one-bit) signal.

**What the Lean claims, taken together, and what they leave to assumptions.** The three theorems establish a negative result, an equality, and a gate property: a cap on output size does not stop one-bit coordination (existence of a perfect one-bit code over a 2^16-value output), an output that does not depend on the secret gives the receiver exactly the blind guess, and a gate that refuses a listed action never lets it execute, whatever signals are used. The first is a witness showing the limits of a cap; the second is nearly definitional; the third holds because the model's only operation is "ask for action a" and the gate filters. The model does not decide which actions count as the malicious one (the `forbidden` list is an input) and it assumes the gate is the only route (`action_gate_complete`, NOT_PROVED), and that zero secret-dependence holds in deployment (`zero_dependence_measured`, NOT_PROVED).

### `ControlStack.SC05.cap_does_not_prevent`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC05Signal.lean:33`
- **Says (plain English):** There exist a sender strategy (for a secret bit, emit a specific one of 65536 possible outputs) and a receiver decoder (read the output and report a bit) such that, for the identity observation of the outputs, the receiver recovers the bit with probability exactly 1. So a limit of 16 bits (65536 distinguishable outputs) per message does not prevent two colluding parties agreeing on a binary choice with certainty.
- **Under these hypotheses:** None beyond the model: the sender is free to emit any of the 65536 outputs, the receiver sees the output exactly, and the secret is a uniformly chosen bit (no shared seed is even needed here).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that any real agents can or do coordinate; it shows only that in the model with a freely choosable, receiver-distinguishable output, a bit passes. (b) Not about the malicious action: coordination is modelled as agreement on a secret bit; no action is executed. (c) It is not a defence claim at all: it is a no-go showing a size cap is not the right control.
- **Kind:** witness / non-vacuity example (existential witness; it instantiates `one_bit_coordinates` at `view = id`, outputs 0 and 1)
- **Non-vacuity:** It is itself the witness.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC05.zero_dependence_is_blind`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC05Signal.lean:40`
- **Says (plain English):** If the distribution of the mediated output (given the shared seed) does not depend on the secret at all, then for every seed distribution and every decoder, the probability of recovering a uniform secret equals exactly 1/|M| (the blind guess rate).
- **Under these hypotheses:** the seed distribution, the output distribution (per seed) and the decoder rows are probability distributions; the output law is a function of the seed only (the secret is not an argument), so "zero secret-dependence" is built into the type of the encoder rather than assumed separately. Whether a real output has zero secret-dependence is world assumption `zero_dependence_measured` (NOT_PROVED).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that real outputs are independent of the secret; the theorem takes this as the shape of the encoder. (b) Not about coordination on a bit: it applies to a uniformly random secret, and if the secret is not uniform the blind rate changes. (c) The conclusion is an equality only for this blind encoder; it does not bound a dependent one.
- **Kind:** general theorem (elementary; the hypothesis that the encoder ignores the secret does most of the work)
- **Non-vacuity:** Any independent output law satisfies the hypotheses; no named witness.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC05.forbidden_never_executes`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC05Signal.lean:89`
- **Says (plain English):** Consider a system whose only operation is "request action a" and whose state is the list of executed actions; the gate refuses any request whose action id is in the `forbidden` list and otherwise appends it. Then, starting from nothing executed, after any sequence of requests no executed action is in the `forbidden` list.
- **Under these hypotheses:** `forbidden` is any list of natural-number action ids and the request sequence is arbitrary (so any signal the colluders use can only influence which ids are requested). The gate is always on in this system (`step forbidden true`). That the real prohibited action can only run through this gate, that actions are correctly identified by id, and that no alias for the same harm exists, are world assumptions (`action_gate_complete`, NOT_PROVED; semantic scope in the file header).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that the real system has no other route to the action, or that "malicious action" is exactly the `forbidden` set: the list is an input chosen outside the model. (b) Not that colluding agents are prevented from coordinating or achieving the same harm via a non-forbidden action. (c) Because the model has exactly one operation and the gate is a filter on it, the property holds by construction; the file also gives `forbidden_executes_without_gate` showing the filter is doing the work.
- **Kind:** general theorem (inductive, by construction of the gate)
- **Non-vacuity:** Not needed in the usual sense (it quantifies over all lists); `forbidden_executes_without_gate` (a `decide` check) shows that with the gate off the action executes.
- **Read depth:** full

## SC-06: Agent plants a covert instruction in shared files, code comments, or other artifacts

**Harm:** An agent's context holds free text written by another agent, or a sensitive action on a value from another agent's artifact runs without a user confirmation of exactly that agent, tool and value.

**What the Lean claims, taken together, and what they leave to assumptions.** Within a small state machine with a typed canonicalising write path, provenance labels on context entries, user confirmations and an admin halt, `sc06_safe` proves (for every operation sequence from the initial state) that no context entry is marked free text and that every executed sensitive action labelled "from another agent" has a recorded matching confirmation by an id in the users list; `halt_freezes` says a halted state never changes. These properties hold because the model's write path discards the free-text field, labels are computed by the model, and the model's confirm/halt operations check the caller id against lists. The gap to the real harm is large and honest: caller identities are supplied by the (adversarial) operation sequence itself, so credential separation is not modelled (`credential_separation`, NOT_PROVED); every typed field is an unbounded natural number, so "typed" values can still carry an instruction; and the origin label is read off by value, as described below.

### `ControlStack.SC06.sc06_safe`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC06Artifacts.lean:242`
- **Says (plain English):** Fix any policy (lists of user ids, admin ids and sensitive tool ids). Run any finite sequence of operations (typed writes, raw writes, reads, user confirmations, actions, halts) from the empty initial state, with the "full" check set (canonicalisation on, single write path, provenance check on, halt check on). Then in the final state (a) no entry in any agent's context is marked as free text, and (b) every executed action whose tool is sensitive and which is labelled "value came from another agent's artifact" has a confirmation record in the final state with the same agent, tool and value, with a confirmation id the action cites, and with a confirming id that is in the policy's users list.
- **Under these hypotheses:** None on the operation sequence (it is fully arbitrary: any caller ids, any ids, any values). The model assumptions that make it true: the typed write path stores no free text (canonicalisation drops it), raw writes are refused, reads produce a context entry whose "from another agent" flag is `writer != reader` where writer is the caller id given to the write operation, and an action reads its "from other" flag from the first context entry of that agent with that value.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) "Confirmed by a user" means only that a `confirm` operation was processed whose caller id is in the users list. Since the operation sequence is arbitrary, nothing in Lean stops the attacking agent's trace from containing a confirm with a user's id, or a write using the victim's id as caller (so the artifact is labelled as the victim's own); this is exactly the unmodelled credential separation. (b) "Typed" fields are arbitrary natural numbers, so the theorem excludes only the model's separate free-text field, not the carrying of an instruction in a typed value (the file notes this is the SC-05 covert channel). (c) The "from another agent" label is attached to the first context entry matching (agent, value); if the same value also entered the reader's context from its own artifact earlier, the action is labelled not-from-other and needs no confirmation, so "a value from another agent's artifact" is true only relative to those labels. Also a confirmation is not consumed: the same confirmation can authorise repeated actions on the same (agent, tool, value).
- **Kind:** general theorem (inductive invariant over the model; true largely by construction of the checks)
- **Non-vacuity:** Shown: `honest_shared_work` (a `decide` check) exhibits a trace where agent 2 reads agent 1's typed value, a user confirms, and the sensitive action runs with a recorded confirmation. Necessity witnesses `raw_path_breaks`, `no_canonicalisation_breaks`, `no_provenance_check_breaks` show the checks matter.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC06.halt_freezes`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC06Artifacts.lean:258`
- **Says (plain English):** With the full check set, if the state is already halted, then running any sequence of operations leaves the state exactly unchanged (no write, read, confirmation or action has any effect, and a second halt changes nothing).
- **Under these hypotheses:** The starting state is marked halted; that is all (the state need not be reachable from the initial one). The halted flag is set only by a `halt` operation whose caller id is in the admins list; that the real halt is performed only by an admin is a credential assumption not modelled.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that halting happens: it says nothing about when or whether a halt is issued. (b) Not that real in-flight actions stop; the model's halt is atomic. (c) Not that agents cannot impersonate an admin to cause a halt (that would be a denial of service rather than a leak, but is also unmodelled).
- **Kind:** general theorem (by construction: every operation is a no-op once halted)
- **Non-vacuity:** `no_halt_check_breaks` shows an action runs after a halt when the halt check is disabled, and not when enabled; no witness that a halt happens beyond that `decide` check.
- **Read depth:** full

## SC-07: Exfiltration of model weights or large sensitive datasets

**Harm:** A sandboxed job sends more than its byte quota off-host, reaches a non-allowlisted destination, or keeps sending after HALT; quantitatively, exfiltrating S information bits through a q-bit per-window quota needs at least ceil(S/q) windows and, with independent per-window catch probability at least p*r and halt on catch, completes before catch with probability at most (1 - p*r)^ceil(S/q).

**What the Lean claims, taken together, and what they leave to assumptions.** There are three layers. (1) Arithmetic and probability: with a per-window cap q, completing S bits needs at least ceil(S/q) windows with traffic, and a plan that completes survives with probability at most (1-pr)^ceil(S/q) if each traffic window is independently caught with probability at least pr; expected leaked bits are at most q/pr. (2) A small gateway model with allowlist, per-window quota and halt, proved safe, with a progress theorem and a link back to the window count. (3) A forward simulation from a model of the nftables forward chain (including header bytes) into the gateway model, transferring safety. Everything about the real world is left as assumptions: the job's only route is the gateway (`single_route`), no encoding through allowed destinations, timing or covert channels (`adversarial_channels`), under-quota uploads complete (`honest_usefulness`), and that the Python/nftables installation behaves like the Lean concrete model (`model_runtime_correspondence`, tested on one host only). Note several places where the quantities modelled differ from the harm statement: the quota counts the size field of a send only, and the per-window independence and per-window recall are premises.

### `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.windows_needed`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Exfil.lean:96`
- **Says (plain English):** If a plan sends a list of per-window bit counts, each at most q (q positive), and the plan's total is at least S, then the number of windows in which any traffic is sent is at least ceil(S/q).
- **Under these hypotheses:** `q > 0`; every per-window count is at most q (the quota enforced); the total is at least S. The numbers are abstract bit counts, so that they are information bits of the secret (rather than bytes or compressible content) is a modelling premise.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not a statement about time or windows being real wall-clock intervals. (b) Not that a real gateway caps each window at q (that is the quota's job; here it is a hypothesis). (c) Not that the secret needs only S bits: if S is measured in raw bytes and the attacker can compress, the bound must be read with the compressed size.
- **Kind:** general theorem (pigeonhole arithmetic)
- **Non-vacuity:** Trivially satisfiable (`no_quota_one_window` and `datacenter_example` use it).
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.sc07_mixed`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Exfil.lean:139`
- **Says (plain English):** Take a random choice of plan (weights `w` over a finite set of plans, each plan a list of per-window bit counts) which is made without any information about the audits. Cap each plan at q bits per window. For each plan define its survival value as the product, over its traffic windows i, of (1 - c i), where c i is the probability that window i's traffic is caught. The weighted sum, over plans whose capped total is at least S, of weight times survival value, is at most (1 - pr)^ceil(S/q), provided every c i lies between pr and 1.
- **Under these hypotheses:** `q > 0`; `0 <= pr <= 1`; each per-window catch probability `c i` is at least `pr` and at most 1; `w` is a probability distribution. The catch probabilities being independent across windows is built into the definition of "survive" (a product), so independence is a premise. That every traffic window, however few bits it carries, is caught with probability at least p*r (audited with probability at least p and detection recall at least r), and that a catch halts the job, are world assumptions. Plans are fixed in advance (the attacker gets no audit information beyond being caught).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) The quantity bounded is "the whole plan is never caught, and the capped total reaches S", not literally "S bits have left before the first catch": a plan that keeps sending after reaching S has to survive extra windows, so the Lean quantity is for the whole plan; the relation to "completes before catch" needs an unproved-in-Lean truncation step (stop at the first window that reaches S). (b) Not a bound on a hidden fixed budget or adaptive attacker (that is `sc07_fixed_budget`). (c) Not about covert, timing or destination-choice channels.
- **Kind:** general theorem
- **Non-vacuity:** `datacenter_example` instantiates the single-plan version with p*r = 0.05; no explicit Lean instance of the full mixed version.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.sc07_fixed_budget`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Exfil.lean:158`
- **Says (plain English):** Suppose exactly B of N windows are chosen uniformly at random as audited, hidden from the attacker, with each audit of a traffic window missing the traffic with probability at most 1 - r given everything the attacker has done and seen. The attacker is adaptive: each window it decides whether to send traffic according to a rule depending on its visible history (traffic decisions and catch flags), and it may keep hidden state. Then the probability (averaged over the audit schedule and the attacker's randomness) that it sends traffic in at least ceil(S/q) windows and is never caught is at most `hypMiss N B ceil(S/q) r`, the hypergeometric miss probability with imperfect recall r.
- **Under these hypotheses:** `B <= N`; `0 <= r <= 1`; for every schedule of size B, the attacker's kernel is non-negative, sums to at most 1 per history, and its probability of a non-caught output with traffic decision `a` is at most (the attacker's own traffic law at that history) times (1 - r if the current window is audited and a is traffic, else 1). That last premise is the recall premise, which must hold per history, not on average (the file header says a population-average detection rate is not enough); the traffic law is independent of the schedule, so the attacker learns nothing about the schedule except catch flags. The numbers S and q enter only through k = ceil(S/q); the statement itself contains no byte or quota condition.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) The connection "completing exfiltration needs at least k traffic windows" is `windows_needed`, which is not part of this statement; the bounded event is "at least k traffic windows and never caught" over exactly N windows. (b) Not that any deployment hides the schedule or achieves recall r per history. (c) It does not apply when the schedule leaks (observable schedule: see `observable_schedule_exfil`).
- **Kind:** refinement/bridge (instance of `ReservedRecall.adaptive_recall_bound`)
- **Non-vacuity:** NOT SHOWN for this instance. The underlying file `ReservedRecall` contains a concrete kernel (`recallK`) satisfying the premises, per its header, but I did not verify it here.
- **Read depth:** full (statement and defs `hypMiss`, `recallSucc`, `AOut`, `memN`, `pU`, `randomSchedules`, `val`); I did not read the proof of `adaptive_recall_bound`.

### `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.expected_leak_le`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Exfil.lean:218`
- **Says (plain English):** For any list of per-window bit counts, capped at q bits per window, and any per-window catch probabilities that are all between `pr` and 1 (with `pr > 0`), the expected number of bits that leave before the first catch is at most q / pr, counting all the bits in the window that gets caught.
- **Under these hypotheses:** `0 < pr`; each `c i` between pr and 1; plans of any length, capped at q per window; catch events independent across windows (built into the definition `expLeak` as a recursion that multiplies by 1 - c i at each traffic window); a window with zero bits is never caught.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not a bound on the probability of leaking a particular S, only an expected value. (b) Not that real windows are independently caught or that the catch halts the job (assumed). (c) Bits in the catching window count as leaked, but covert encodings that use zero-size sends are not part of the plan model.
- **Kind:** general theorem
- **Non-vacuity:** Trivially satisfiable (any plan and constant `c`); no named witness.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.observable_schedule_exfil`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Exfil.lean:187`
- **Says (plain English):** For any positive q, the plan that sends 0, q, 0, q bits in four consecutive windows, when windows 0 and 2 are audited with certainty and windows 1 and 3 never, leaks 2q bits in total and is never caught (its survival value is exactly 1).
- **Under these hypotheses:** `q > 0` and the specific catch probabilities 1 at windows 0 and 2, 0 elsewhere.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not a bound on anything: it is a counterexample showing that if the audit schedule is known to the attacker the exfiltration bound fails. (b) Says nothing about how likely a schedule is to leak in any deployment.
- **Kind:** negative control (shows a weakened model fails); also a finite computation
- **Non-vacuity:** Itself a concrete witness.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC07Exfil.datacenter_example`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Exfil.lean:197`
- **Says (plain English):** With S = 8 x 10^11 bits (100 GB), a per-window quota of 8 x 10^9 bits, and every window's catch probability between 0.05 and 1, the number ceil(S/q) equals 100 and any plan whose capped total is at least S has survival value at most 0.006 (since 0.95^100 is below 0.006).
- **Under these hypotheses:** the capped plan's total reaches 800000000000 bits; each per-window catch probability is at least 0.05 and at most 1; independence across windows (in the definition of survival).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that real audits catch 5% of traffic windows. (b) Not about compressed or partial exfiltration: the target is 100 GB of information (weights could be reconstructable from less). (c) The same whole-plan-survival caveat as `sc07_mixed`.
- **Kind:** finite computation (decide/native) combined with an instance of `sc07_plan_bound`; numeric corollary
- **Non-vacuity:** The hypotheses on `c` and `b` are satisfiable (for example `c = 0.05` and a plan sending the quota 100 times), but no Lean instance is named.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC07Liveness.sc07_safe`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Liveness.lean:169`
- **Says (plain English):** In a model gateway with an allowlist of destinations, a per-window quota q, tick operations that open new windows, and an admin halt, run any sequence of sends (to any destination, of any size), ticks and halts from the initial state with all three checks on. Then every forwarded send went to an allowlisted destination, and for every window the total bits forwarded in that window is at most q. (A send that would push the window over q is dropped whole.)
- **Under these hypotheses:** None on the operation sequence. In the model, "bits" is only the size field `n` of each send; a send of zero bits to an allowlisted destination is forwarded and consumes no quota.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) The total over the whole run is not bounded by q: ticks open new windows and are ordinary adversary-chosen operations in the model (the later theorem `total_le` bounds the total by q times the number of windows opened). So "never sends more than its byte quota" holds per window only. (b) The quota counts payload size only; the destination chosen among allowlisted ones, the number and order of (possibly zero-size) sends, and timing carry information the model does not count (these are `adversarial_channels`). (c) Nothing about halt here: halting is a separate theorem.
- **Kind:** general theorem
- **Non-vacuity:** Shown: `honest_upload` (a `decide` check) forwards an 8-bit upload in two chunks; `quota_off_exceeds` and `allowlist_off_leaks` are negative controls showing the checks matter.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC07Liveness.upload_completes`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Liveness.lean:197`
- **Says (plain English):** If the gateway is not halted, the destination is allowlisted, and the bits already used in the current window plus the total of the upload's chunks do not exceed q, then sending the chunks one after another forwards every chunk in order in the current window: the log after the run is the old log followed by one entry per chunk.
- **Under these hypotheses:** the starting state is any state (it need not be reachable) with halted false; destination allowlisted; used + total of chunks <= q. The run consists only of the upload's own sends, with nothing interleaved.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not progress under interleaving: other jobs' traffic or ticks between chunks are not part of the run; the scenario's note "progress under interleaving" is not what this statement proves, and the witness `shared_quota_blocks` shows interleaved traffic blocking an honest upload. (b) Not that the real runtime completes byte-exact uploads (that is `honest_usefulness`, NOT_PROVED, and the runtime's quota counts wire bytes including headers). (c) Not that an upload larger than the remaining quota completes.
- **Kind:** general theorem
- **Non-vacuity:** `honest_upload` (decide) and `fresh_window_upload` in the same file.
- **Read depth:** full

### `ControlStack.SC07Liveness.exfil_needs_windows`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Liveness.lean:228`
- **Says (plain English):** For a positive per-window quota q, after any operation sequence from the initial state, if the total number of forwarded bits is at least S, then ceil(S/q) is at most the window counter plus one (that is, at least ceil(S/q) windows have been opened, counting the initial one).
- **Under these hypotheses:** `q > 0`; arbitrary operation sequence; the total forwarded is at least S. It is derived from the model's own quota step (via `total_le`).
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Not that windows correspond to wall-clock time or to independently audited intervals: a new window is opened by a `tick` operation which the arbitrary trace may issue at will, so the link to the per-window catch probability in `SC07Exfil` is by modelling convention. (b) Not that a catch happens in any window.
- **Kind:** refinement/bridge (connects the gateway model to `windows_needed`'s premise)
- **Non-vacuity:** Trivial.

### `ControlStack.SC07Refinement.concrete_safe`  — `ControlStack/Scenarios/SC07Refinement.lean:272`
- **Says (plain English):** In a concrete model of the gateway's forward chain (allowlisted (ip, port) pairs, a quota object with a kernel-style `consumed` counter that adds each packet's on-wire length and then drops the packet if `consumed` has reached the quota threshold Q, and a halt that replaces the chain with a drop rule): for any sequence of packets (to any address and port, any payload and header sizes), quota resets and halts, starting from the initial state and with the quota rule present, every forwarded packet went to an allowlisted (ip, port), and for every window the total forwarded payload bytes is at most Q - 1.
- **Under these hypotheses:** the quota rule is present in the configuration (`quotaRule = true`); no restriction on the packet/reset/halt sequence. The model's trusted-kernel semantics (exact installed rules, `consumed` accounting, crossing packet dropped, header bytes counted) are the assumption `model_runtime_correspondence`, tested only on one host.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) The bound is per window, and `resetQuota` is an operation in the arbitrary sequence; in the runtime the quota is per run (one window), but the Lean statement does not bound the total across resets. (b) Not that the Python gateway installs this ruleset or that the kernel behaves as modelled (tested, not proved). (c) Not about return traffic, retransmission, or covert channels; payload bytes only, and with Q = 0 the natural subtraction gives 0 and every packet is dropped.
- **Kind:** refinement/bridge (transfers `SC07Liveness.sc07_safe` through a forward simulation)
- **Non-vacuity:** Shown: `quota_rule_absent_breaks` (no quota rule: 24 bytes leave; with it, 8) and `crossing_packet_dropped` (both `decide`).
- **Read depth:** full

- **Says (plain English):** With the quota rule present and a positive threshold Q, for every packet/reset/halt sequence and every window, the total on-wire bytes (payload plus header) of the forwarded packets in that window is strictly less than Q.
- **Under these hypotheses:** `quotaRule = true`, `Q > 0`; same model-correspondence assumptions as `concrete_safe`.
- **Does NOT say:** (a) Per window only, and quota resets are allowed in the trace, so not a bound on the whole run. (b) Not that the real kernel behaves this way (the model records that the crossing packet is itself dropped, which matches the preregistration's calibration claim).
